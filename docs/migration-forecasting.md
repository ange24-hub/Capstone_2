# Verified arrival, departure and net-migration forecasting

Approved **Municipal LGU administrators** own prediction analytics. Open **Reports → Prediction analytics** (`/municipal/prediction-analytics/forecast`) for actual-record Seasonal naive forecasts of arrivals, departures and signed net migration, a six-month forecast graph/table, source coverage and held-out error checks. **Migration Trends → Verified forecast** uses the same report and model. **RBI report history** (`/municipal/rbi-report-history`) provides the all-barangay source inventory and links to entry classification and arrival-count reviews, without alternative models, sample forecasts or age-estimated analytics. Barangay secretaries continue to prepare/review their own data, but cannot open forecasting or the municipal history inventory, including direct URLs. The assistant withholds numerical forecasts from non-admin accounts. Predictions count movement events, not unique people or people predicted to move. Net migration excludes births and deaths; it is not total population change. No individual-resident risk model is used.

## Separate RBI history review and experiments

Research/demo screens are absent from the normal navigation and production routes. Their archived routes (`/migration-monitoring/demo`, `/migration-monitoring/history-experiment` and `/municipal/prediction-analytics`) exist only in `local` and `testing` environments so earlier research remains reproducible without appearing in the online system. Generated prediction reports and user-provided experiment CSVs are excluded from Git releases and Docker build context. The descriptions below document those archived development experiments; they do not change the production Seasonal naive model or its reviewed inputs.

The separate history-experiment inventory reads the same `new_inhabitants` imported/saved/consolidated history and `barangay_rbi_updates` monthly forms used on every secretary's Report History page. It includes drafts and submitted forms; read access does not sign, submit, confirm or rewrite them. It does not replace the reviewed, submitted arrival counts used by actual-record forecasts. Ownership maps forms to barangays, with explicit conflicts flagged for review. Linked history IDs and identity keys prevent a saved form from counting its imported resident twice. Multiple monthly forms, duplicate identities, incomplete classifications and unknown reporting months block the affected aggregate rather than creating a false municipal total.

In **Complete municipal coverage** mode, every configured barangay is required. Municipal counts align by calendar month, not by concatenating reports from different barangays. A month is complete only when every barangay has a usable count. Missing months strictly between that barangay's recorded endpoints are zero under the stated reporting convention; absence of the barangay's history or months outside its coverage remain unknown. Partial known subtotals are shown separately. Legacy labels with an explicit year, such as `SEPTEMBER 10, 2024`, can supply their recorded month. A bare `JUNE` never acquires a guessed reporting year. Undated and not-yet-completed records remain outside training. The page defaults to the separate **Date-complete records only** sample described under Validation below.

The latest six months in a consecutive complete municipal source window are reserved for historical testing. The initial fit uses all earlier months in that window, with at least six initial observations. No target enters its own or an earlier fit. Rolling one-month evaluation may use an earlier test actual for a later prediction after it would have become known; it is not a fixed six-month-ahead evaluation. The screen shows both date ranges, per-origin cutoffs, actual/predicted counts and MAE, with the mean-rate comparator on identical dates. An incomplete source window displays readiness and review links instead of fabricated forecasts or accuracy percentages.

**Confirmed classifications** is the municipal-coverage default. The dated-record sample defaults to **Age-based preliminary estimates**: under 12 months old at the end of the report month suggests probable newborn; older entries suggest probable in-migration. Admins can select either basis explicitly. Only unclassified entries with a valid identity, birth date and report month receive this fallback. Existing confirmed infant migrants, newborns and late registrations keep their saved classifications. Invalid explicit classifications stay pending. Age-based targets are a derived assumption-based series, not independent ground truth for migration accuracy; the page labels them accordingly. No resident or source classification is changed by either preview. The per-barangay history review page displays saved classification and age suggestion side by side.

## Registry movement and RBI arrival sources

Manage person or household movement from **Resident Registry → Movement**. “Moved out” keeps the person's active registration in Consolidated, sets the residence to living elsewhere, removes them from Living in Barangay, and lists them in Moved Out. “Transferred” archives their registration: they leave Consolidated and Living in Barangay while remaining in Moved Out. A dated departure is stored in the existing movement ledger used for out-migration counts. Moving from moved out to transferred updates that departure rather than counting a second move. Undated imported statuses do not invent movement dates. Returning to living here preserves previous dated departure history; a later genuine departure creates a new event.

Movement changes require an actual date, an explicit confirmation, and current record versions. Household changes apply atomically to all active registered members of that household. Living in Barangay and Moved Out are review lists; their old write endpoints redirect to Resident Registry. Free-text remarks do not silently transfer registration. Changes are logged in Registry Activity History.

For arrivals, open **RBI Forms → 3-month arrival counts**. Consecutive, non-overlapping reporting periods start with the earliest dated Report History entry or submitted reporting month for that barangay: for example February–April, May–July, August–October. The period must have ended before review. Under the barangay's reporting convention, a month with no report or saved entries between two recorded months means zero new entries: March and May reports with no April report supply April = 0. Drafts, reopened forms and saved history entries awaiting classification/submission never become zeros. Months before the first or after the last recorded month stay unknown. Adding a form or saved entry to a previously empty gap invalidates its review. The form retains each month's count instead of distributing a quarterly total artificially.

For a month with no new or deceased entries, use **Report history → Month with no new or deceased residents?** to create a zero-activity draft. It requires explicit confirmation and normal signatures/submission. It refuses to replace an existing monthly form. A draft alone is not a verified zero.

Each RBI resident entry records its entry type: Birth / Newborn, In-migration or Late registration. Only confirmed In-migration entries with an arrival month matching the report are eligible. Enter the previous residence when known; when an in-migrant is confirmed but the origin address was not recorded, explicitly check Previous residence not recorded. This flag preserves the missing origin instead of inventing an address. An exact arrival day is optional when historical records only establish the month; no day is invented. A baby who actually moved from another place can be an in-migrant. Birth dates alone do not classify migration. Unknown or invalid classifications block review. Repeated arrivals within the period and marked demo rows are excluded. The reviewer confirms the monthly counts, including history-gap zeros; counts cannot exceed the eligible entries. Submitted RBI forms and reviewed zero-entry history gaps are the arrival source; legacy `in` movement events and legacy arrival verification fields do not establish arrival readiness.

The existing Report History table lists dated `NewInhabitant` records for the selected barangay. **Prepare / open classification form** creates a draft for that exact month, preserves source record IDs, and leaves the original records intact. Expand Review each resident entry to see the source names and birth dates, then use Review this resident to open the corresponding form card. Unknown entries may remain Needs review while a draft is saved; they continue to block the arrival-period review. Classify and submit the completed review through the RBI form workflow. Existing forms are opened without replacement, and the legacy raw-history submit action refuses to overwrite a prepared or classified form. Entries without a recorded reporting month remain excluded until their source month is confirmed.

The additive `rbi_arrival_periods` table stores reviewed monthly counts, source fingerprints, reviewer, review time and notes. Editing or reopening a submitted source invalidates its review on the next read. Reopen an arrival review to exclude it immediately until rechecked. A new earlier submitted month may change the reporting-period anchor and requires affected reviews again. Approved secretaries can review only their assigned barangay; approved municipal staff select a barangay. Reviews and reopenings are audited.

Departure completeness is confirmed separately in **Migration trends → Forecast → Check the records used**. Existing departure checks remain valid when their source fingerprints match. The old nullable arrival columns in `migration_reporting_periods` are preserved for upgrade compatibility, but forecasts read arrivals from the new RBI period reviews. A municipal forecast requires complete history from every configured barangay. Review fingerprints prevent stale confirmations; duplicate departure events block monthly verification. No confirmation changes or deletes resident or movement records.

Demo departure events marked by the demo reason, resident marker or `DEMO-MIGRATION-V1` household remain excluded from model fitting. Synthetic analysis is available only in the local/testing archive.

## Model fitting and evaluation

The native PHP `SeasonalNaiveForecast` service fits the latest twelve verified monthly counts on demand. It is shared with the Seasonal naive CSV experiment so the forecasting rule stays identical. The live report reads only actual database sources; saved CSV counts, example scores and model-result JSON files are never substituted for municipal or barangay records. Python, Ollama and external prediction APIs are not required.

The input is the latest uninterrupted run of complete, unchanged reporting months from reviewed RBI arrival periods and the dated Moved Out departure ledger, ending at the last completed calendar quarter. Source windows refresh every three months: January 1, April 1, July 1 and October 1, on the first request in the new quarter. No scheduler is required. Within a quarter, the source cutoff and fixed method stay unchanged; the next-calendar-month target advances each month. Missing, reopened, stale or duplicate-affected months break the verified run immediately. A municipal aggregate requires matching complete months from every configured barangay. The review window is 36 completed months through the quarter cutoff; the final fitted profile uses its latest twelve consecutive verified months. Corrections invalidate reviews immediately rather than waiting for the next quarter.

The admin's fixed method is **Seasonal naive (same month last year)**: a target repeats the count from the matching calendar month in the most recent training year. Its annual-repeat assumption is explicit, not a guarantee that migration is seasonal or that a previous CSV score will hold on live data. No parameters or methods are selected using test outcomes. The original multi-candidate `MigrationForecastEngine` remains available to the separate historical/demo experiments.

Arrivals and departures fit their own reviewed counts at the same cutoff and horizon. **Net = predicted arrivals − predicted departures**, preserving negative values. Both components must be ready for a net forecast. Net error is measured only when both have matching held-out target months; missing test history yields an available net estimate with unmeasured error, while conflicting nonempty test windows are rejected. Net MAE is calculated after subtraction, not by adding component MAEs. The benchmark subtracts the two recursive three-month moving-average predictions. Better, equal and worse comparisons remain visible.

Forecast readiness and error readiness are separate. Twelve consecutive complete verified months support a forecast; no missing month is invented as zero. For a horizon of `h` months, three separate historical test targets require `12 + (h - 1) + 3` consecutive verified months. Each test prefix ends `h` months before its target. The fixed Seasonal naive prediction and recursive three-month moving-average comparator use that same prefix and horizon. A current/later test actual never enters its own fit. Without enough history for all three tests, MAE and baseline MAE remain null, and the UI/assistant explicitly say the error has not been measured. They never display zero error by substituting null.

- Current-year view: the first target is the **next calendar month**, using the previous quarter's cutoff. Twelve verified months support the point estimate; three error measurements require 16, 17 or 18 months for the quarter's two-, three- or four-month horizons respectively. In October, November and December, source history ends in September and the first targets are November, December and January. The six-month graph/table uses that same fixed source profile for the following six targets. Reference-month dates are displayed for review.
- Historical-year view: source history ends in December of that year and the first target is the following January. Twelve verified months support the estimate; fifteen are required for the three one-month historical error checks. A twelve-month zero history therefore forecasts zero but does not claim zero measured error.
- Future reporting years: no estimate is produced.

These minimums apply to each series and are implementation readiness gates, not guarantees of statistical reliability. The dashboard shows mean absolute error (MAE), moving-average baseline MAE, exact held-out actual/predicted counts, and comparison charts for every available series. One component may be available while net remains unavailable. It reports when forecasts match or perform worse than the baseline instead of claiming universal improvement. Three held-out months are limited evidence; verify more history and prospective results before operational reliance. MAE is an error in event counts, not an accuracy percentage or a calibrated confidence interval. More advanced models or software tests passing are not evidence of improved real-world forecasting accuracy.

The assistant and read-only audit use this same scoped Seasonal naive report. The assistant gives next-calendar-month arrivals, departures and net in English/Bisaya, and explicitly marks unavailable held-out errors. A net request never silently substitutes departures. Retrieved source records are not sent to a language model.

## Installation and audits

Normal installation includes both forecast migrations. Existing Docker deployments run `php artisan migration:prepare-forecast-storage` at startup when general migrations are disabled. This idempotent command applies only the reporting table migration and `2026_11_05_010000_add_arrival_verification_to_migration_reporting_periods.php`, which adds nullable arrival verification fields. Existing backups, database identities, keys and volumes stay in place.

For an existing non-Docker installation:

```sh
php artisan migration:prepare-forecast-storage
php artisan migration:forecast-audit
php artisan migration:forecast-audit --barangay=16
```

The audit is read-only and outputs aggregate coverage, excluded demo totals and available evaluation; it contains no resident names, addresses or coordinates. Local results are not evidence of the hosted database's contents. `scripts/check-migration-prediction.php` now invokes this aggregate audit. `start-rbim.bat` prepares reporting storage and no longer launches the legacy Python predictor.

### Bayesian Poisson alternative with a fixed Jeffreys prior

The historical experiment's **Method** selector also offers **Bayesian Poisson (Jeffreys prior)** for the same six-/twelve-month training windows and one-/three-/six-month forecasts. It assumes a constant arrival rate and independent, equally exposed monthly counts. The fixed reference prior is proportional to `1/sqrt(rate)`. After observing `S` arrivals in `n` training months, the posterior has Gamma shape `S + 0.5` and rate `n`, and the point forecast is its mean `(S + 0.5) / n`. The prior is not proper on its own; the posterior is proper for these nonempty training windows. The adjustment is a model assumption, not an actual arrival, resident, demo record or changed source count. It is fixed in code and is never tuned using held-out actuals. See [Duke Statistics, Notes 10, page 4](https://www2.stat.duke.edu/~st118/sta250/priors.pdf) and [Columbia's Poisson–Gamma conjugate analysis](https://www.columbia.edu/~cjd11/charles_dimaggio/DIRE/styled-4/styled-11/code-4/).

For Cabascan's June–November 2020 all-zero training window, this produces `0.5/6 = 0.083333...` expected arrivals in each December 2020–May 2021 month, or `0.5` in total. Actuals remain `4, 2, 0, 0, 1, 7`. MAE is `2.305555...`, compared with `2.333333...` for the zero mean-rate forecast: a small difference of `0.027777...` arrivals per month, not an accuracy percentage. Both miss most later arrivals. This alternative was introduced after the earlier historical results were inspected, so its apparent improvement is exploratory and needs unseen future data for validation. It is not automatically chosen using the test scores. When it performs worse in another window, that result remains visible.

Use `?barangay_id=5&mode=holdout&method=poisson_jeffreys&training_months=6&horizon=6&end_month=2020-12`, or run `php artisan migration:history-experiment --barangay=5 --mode=holdout --method=poisson_jeffreys --training-months=6 --horizon=6 --end-month=2020-12`. The private result retains prior/posterior parameters, unchanged observed counts, both model scores and the source fingerprint. All six forecasts use the November cutoff, even after December actuals are available. Missing actuals remain unknown, and incomplete training blocks the fit. The existing mean-rate option and live verified forecasting engine remain available and unchanged in their fitting rules.

### Expanding-window rolling backtesting

Open **Migration trends → Rolling backtesting**. Cabascan defaults to the requested December 2020–May 2021 test interval, with June–November 2020 as six initial training months and Bayesian Poisson with the fixed Jeffreys prior as the displayed method. The mean-rate baseline is evaluated on exactly the same prefixes. Other windows can be chosen in the form; **First test month** uses the existing `end_month` query parameter. Supported initial windows are six or twelve months, followed by one, three or six monthly tests. This is an expanding window, not a sliding window of constant length.

December's estimate uses June–November only. Once December's actual is available, January's fit uses June–December; this continues through May, whose fit ends in April. The method and prior stay fixed for the entire retrospective comparison. Only each prefix's rate is re-estimated. A current or later target actual cannot affect its own or an earlier prediction. Earlier test actuals may affect subsequent predictions once they would have been known. Every initial and test month must have a complete classified count; an unknown month blocks the experiment instead of supplying a fabricated zero. Reports remain unchanged, and no official verification is written.

The Cabascan actuals are `4, 2, 0, 0, 1, 7`. Bayesian point forecasts are `0.5/6, 4.5/7, 6.5/8, 6.5/9, 6.5/10, 7.5/11`; each training prefix grows from six to eleven months. The page shows the actual-versus-predicted graph, model MAE, rolling mean-rate MAE, and a monthly audit table with actuals, predictions, training cutoffs and absolute errors. MAE is computed using unrounded predictions. Neither this exploratory historical comparison nor its one-month error is proof of future accuracy or of six-month-ahead performance.

Run `php artisan migration:history-experiment --barangay=5 --mode=rolling --method=poisson_jeffreys --training-months=6 --horizon=6 --end-month=2020-12`. Private `result.json` records the protocol, source fingerprint, initial training dates, six test dates and per-origin prior/posterior parameters. `test-results.csv` contains each actual, prediction, rolling mean-rate baseline, absolute error and training cutoff. It has no duplicated final holdout or separate fixed-origin future forecast. Existing fixed-origin and short-history experiments remain accessible. Evaluation follows [time series cross-validation](https://otexts.com/fpp3/toolbox.html#time-series-cross-validation).

### Separate synthetic training experiment

Run `php scripts/train-demo-forecast.php` to fit and evaluate the same PHP forecasting engine using a reproducible, explicitly synthetic dataset. This standalone runner does not bootstrap Laravel, access the database, confirm reporting periods, update the legacy Python artifact, or change live forecasts.

Each run creates a new private `storage/app/forecast-demo/` folder containing 108 monthly rows (three fictional scenarios, 36 months each), evaluation metrics, held-out predictions, a JSON result manifest with checksums, and a readable report. Scenarios cover stable counts, a gradual trend, and an abrupt change. A fixed seed makes comparisons reproducible; the counts are not estimated from actual residents.

The experiment selects methods using earlier rolling validation at the dashboard's four-month selection horizon. It evaluates July–September 2026 targets at a three-month horizon, using April–June 2026 training cutoffs respectively. It then refits on all 36 months for a December 2026 synthetic forecast. The final three target actuals never choose the model. The in-memory completeness flags are simulation inputs, not staff attestations, and are never stored in the reporting database.

Read test MAE alongside the moving-average baseline and retain worse/equal results. MAE measures error in event counts, not an accuracy percentage. These three held-out targets per scenario demonstrate the workflow only; they cannot establish performance on actual barangay data. Do not import the demo CSV as verified production records.

Approved Municipal LGU administrators can open **Migration trends → Demo prediction analysis** (`/migration-monitoring/demo`). This separate read-only page shows the saved experiment at `resources/demo/migration-forecast.json`, with scenario tabs, clearly labeled synthetic actual-versus-predicted graphs, moving-average comparisons, signed net forecasts, and exact test values. The December 2026 target is a fixed experiment date, not a live next-month estimate. Real records and reporting confirmations are never read or modified by the demo controller. Screenshots should retain the **DEMO DATA ONLY** banner and graph legend. To update the displayed experiment, rerun the script and explicitly review/copy its JSON result into that resource; deployment never runs training automatically.

`MigrationForecastEngineTest` checks held-out leakage, trend fitting, known zeros, chronological continuity and MAE arithmetic. `MigrationNetForecastTest` verifies signed net arithmetic, matched targets and independently measured net errors, including worse-than-baseline results. `NetMigrationForecastTest` exercises joint readiness with submitted RBI arrival reviews, source invalidation, legacy-arrival exclusion, municipal completeness, English/Bisaya answers and preservation of departure checks. `RegistryMovementWorkflowTest` and `RbiArrivalPeriodTest` cover movement state transitions, household atomicity, actual dates, rolling reporting periods, newborn/repeat exclusion, stale reviews, and barangay isolation. `VerifiedMigrationForecastTest` retains departure compatibility and quarterly rollover coverage. The existing dashboard/report tests still protect role and year filters. Model accuracy on fixture data is not evidence of accuracy on real barangay records.

Evaluation follows the time-ordered validation principles in [Forecasting: Principles and Practice](https://otexts.com/fpp3/tscv.html).
